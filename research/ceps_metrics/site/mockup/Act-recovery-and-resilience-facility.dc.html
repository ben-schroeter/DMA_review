Legal instruments / Industrial Policy

Recovery and Resilience Facility

Regulation (EU) 2021/241 ↗Adopted 12 Feb 2021Evidence: moderate

Proposed 28 May 2020 and adopted 12 Feb 2021 under the first von der Leyen Commission (Margrethe Vestager, Thierry Breton) · Part of the Digital Decade agenda

What it does

Establishes a temporary facility to help Member States recover from the COVID-19 crisis and become more resilient, supporting the green and digital transitions. It funds reforms and investments set out in national recovery and resilience plans, released when agreed milestones and targets are met; plans must devote at least 20% to digital objectives. It provides up to EUR 312.5 billion in grants and EUR 360 billion in loans (2018 prices).

What we know so far

The Recovery and Resilience Facility paid out EUR 450 billion (79% of its funds) by October 2026, tied to reforms and investments that countries promised, of which about two-thirds of milestones were met. Commission models suggested it could lift EU output by up to 1.4% by 2026, while ECB staff now expect a smaller 0.4-0.9% for the euro area because of widespread delays. EU auditors warn that the fund's indicators track spending progress, not real results, so its actual impact remains unmeasured until the 2028 ex-post evaluation.

Objectives and key results

Objectives are the aims the Commission set for the law. Key results are how it said it would measure them: indicators in the proposal and forecasts in the impact assessment, checked against the evidence available in October 2026.
