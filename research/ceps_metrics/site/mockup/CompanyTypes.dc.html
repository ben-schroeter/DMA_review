Findings · By type of company

What the rules mean for different kinds of company

EU digital law does not fall evenly. We grouped companies into six types by what they do, and counted the obligations that apply to each type on top of the rules every company follows.

Rules for platforms, cloud and AI barely existed before 2018

Makers of connected products and financial firms carry the most type-specific obligations, about 3,718 and 3,413, but both already had sizeable rulebooks in 2018. For platforms, cloud and AI the rules are almost entirely new: gatekeepers and very large platforms went from 21 specific obligations at the start of 2018 to 1,452, and cloud, data and AI providers from none to 1,358. Every company also follows a baseline of 1,987 obligations, from data protection to consumer, tax and company law.

Obligations by type of company over time

The baseline that every company follows has roughly doubled since 2018. The type-specific layers have grown far faster, and most of the growth came after 2020: the Digital Services and Digital Markets Acts for platforms, the AI Act and NIS 2 for cloud and AI, MiCA and DORA for finance, and the Machinery, Toy Safety and Cyber Resilience rules for product makers.

Six types of company

One company can be several types. Amazon, for example, is a gatekeeper and a very large online marketplace, one of the largest cloud providers through AWS, and a maker of connected devices; each layer brings its own rules.

How we assigned the rules

For each of the 105 adopted laws we coded which types it applies to, from its scope provisions: in full, or only in part. Where a law has tiers, we used its articles: in the Digital Services Act, for example, Articles 11 to 15 apply to all intermediary services, 16 to 18 to hosting services, 19 to 32 to online platforms and marketplaces, and 33 to 43 to very large platforms only. Elsewhere we counted the obligations whose wording names that kind of company. The baseline covers data protection, consumer law, VAT, company law, competition and intellectual property. Laws addressed to public bodies and EU funding programmes, whose company obligations are grant conditions, are not counted for any type; nor are trust service providers under eIDAS, a specialised group outside the six types.

These are profiles of types of company, not legal assessments of named firms: what applies to a given firm depends on its activities, size and designations. Company names are illustrations; gatekeeper and very-large-platform status follows the Commission's designations. The full table of laws by type is in the data download.
