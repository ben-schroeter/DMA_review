European Electronic Communications Code

Directive (EU) 2018/1972 ↗Adopted 11 Dec 2018Evidence: thin

Proposed 14 Sep 2016 and adopted 11 Dec 2018 under the Juncker Commission (Andrus Ansip) · Part of the Digital Single Market agenda

What it does

Establishes a harmonised EU framework for regulating electronic communications networks and services. It aims to build an internal market that encourages very high capacity networks, sustainable competition, security and end-user benefits, and to ensure affordable, good-quality services. It sets the tasks of national regulators, rules on authorisation, access, radio spectrum and universal service, and lays down end-user rights.

What we know so far

The telecoms code was forecast to lift EU GDP by 1.45% by 2025 by spurring fibre and 5G investment; that has never been measured. Fast networks did spread - fibre reached 69% of homes and 5G 94% in 2024 - but the Commission's 2026 review found its main investment tool (co-investment) was never used and national rules stayed fragmented, leading it to propose replacing the code with a Digital Networks Act. Older research shows strict access regulation tends to reduce incumbents' network investment.

Who it applies to

Size thresholds and exemptions: Microenterprises offering only number-independent communications services (such as messaging apps) are exempt from most end-user rights rules in Title III, except Articles 99 and 100 (Article 98).
