Information Society (InfoSoc) Directive

Directive 2001/29/EC ↗Adopted 22 May 2001Evidence: thin

Proposed 10 Dec 1997 under the Santer Commission (Martin Bangemann) and adopted 22 May 2001 under the Prodi Commission (Erkki Liikanen) · Part of the Market opening agenda

What it does

Aims to harmonise copyright and related rights in the internal market, with particular attention to the information society. It grants authors and other rightholders exclusive rights of reproduction, communication to the public and distribution, sets out an exhaustive list of permitted exceptions, and requires legal protection against the circumvention of technological protection measures and the removal of rights-management information.

What we know so far

The InfoSoc Directive promised to harmonise copyright across the EU and spur online content services, but set no measurable targets. The Commission's own 2007 evaluation found it harmonised copyright exceptions only 'very modestly' and created legal uncertainty around copy protection (DRM), and in 2016 the Commission proposed new mandatory exceptions to fix the same problems. Independent research suggests restrictive exceptions are linked to less data-mining research and that removing DRM raised music sales by about 10%, but no study measures the Directive's overall economic effect.

Objectives and key results

Objectives are the aims the Commission set for the law. Key results are how it said it would measure them: indicators in the proposal and forecasts in the impact assessment, checked against the evidence available in October 2026.

  • Growth and innovation in online content services
