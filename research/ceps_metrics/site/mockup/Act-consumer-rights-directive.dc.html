Consumer Rights Directive

Directive 2011/83/EU ↗Adopted 25 Oct 2011Evidence: thin

Proposed 8 Oct 2008 under the first Barroso Commission (Viviane Reding) and adopted 25 Oct 2011 under the second Barroso Commission (Neelie Kroes) · Part of the Lisbon agenda

What it does

Aims to achieve a high level of consumer protection and contribute to the internal market by harmonising aspects of contracts between consumers and traders. It requires traders to give consumers key pre-contractual information, gives a 14-day right of withdrawal for distance and off-premises contracts, and sets rules on delivery, risk, payment fees and additional charges.

What we know so far

The Consumer Rights Directive harmonised pre-contract information and the 14-day right to withdraw from online and doorstep sales from 2014. Its promised gain - cheaper cross-border selling - was never measured: the Commission's 2017 review found too little data, and the UK's forecast GBP 2.6 billion benefit was not checked after the fact. Reviews show compliance costs are modest but withdrawal rights remain the top cross-border complaint, and the 2024 review flagged subscription traps and simplification needs.
