Open Internet Access Regulation

Regulation (EU) 2015/2120 ↗Adopted 25 Nov 2015Evidence: thin

Proposed 11 Sep 2013 under the second Barroso Commission (Neelie Kroes) and adopted 25 Nov 2015 under the Juncker Commission (Andrus Ansip) · Part of the Europe 2020 agenda

What it does

Establishes common rules to safeguard equal and non-discriminatory treatment of traffic in internet access services and related end-user rights. It requires providers to treat all traffic equally, subject to limited reasonable traffic management, and to be transparent about speeds. It also creates a retail pricing mechanism to abolish roaming surcharges within the EU.

What we know so far

The EU's net neutrality rules set no measurable targets; Commission reviews in 2019 and 2023 found them applied consistently and still fit for purpose, and EU court rulings in 2020-21 ended most 'zero-rating' offers. Evidence on economic effects is limited and conflicting: one study of 32 OECD countries finds strict net neutrality rules slowed fibre investment, while studies of zero-rating disagree on whether it raised mobile data prices.

Objectives and key results

Objectives are the aims the Commission set for the law. Key results are how it said it would measure them: indicators in the proposal and forecasts in the impact assessment, checked against the evidence available in October 2026.

  • Single market for electronic communications (whole package)
  • Investment by internet access providers
