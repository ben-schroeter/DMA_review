Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA)

Regulation (EU) 2023/1114 ↗Adopted 31 May 2023Evidence: thin

Proposed 24 Sep 2020 and adopted 31 May 2023 under the first von der Leyen Commission (Margrethe Vestager, Thierry Breton) · Part of the Digital Decade agenda

What it does

Lays down uniform EU rules for offering crypto-assets to the public and admitting them to trading, including asset-referenced tokens and e-money tokens, and for crypto-asset service providers. It requires transparency and disclosure through white papers, authorisation and supervision of issuers and service providers, protection of holders and clients, and measures against insider dealing and market manipulation.

What we know so far

MiCA set up EU licences for crypto firms and stablecoin issuers. By October 2026 about 365 firms were licensed and licensed euro stablecoins had more than doubled in value to about USD 674 million, though from a tiny base. Early studies find MiCA moved EU exchanges from unlicensed to licensed stablecoins but barely changed wider markets; promised savings on remittances and reductions in fraud have not been measured.

Objectives and key results

Objectives are the aims the Commission set for the law. Key results are how it said it would measure them: indicators in the proposal and forecasts in the impact assessment, checked against the evidence available in October 2026.

Objectives
  • Remove regulatory obstacles to issuance, trading and post-trading of crypto-assets; increase company funding through ICOs/STOs; limit fraud and illicit practices; give consumers and investors access to new investment and payment instruments
