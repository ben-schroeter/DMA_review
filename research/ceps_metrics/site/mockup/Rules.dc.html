More than half of today's digital laws date from 2020 or later

Regulations, which apply directly in every member state, now outnumber directives almost two to one. Since 2020 the new acts have spread across almost every policy area, led by industrial policy, research and connectivity.

Rules aimed directly at companies have grown fastest since 2018

About 7,800 restrictions name a private party, such as an undertaking, provider, platform or controller, as the one who must act: 4,672 in the main acts and 3,120 in their implementing and delegated acts. The average new act binds companies directly 73 times, against 20 in the 2000s.

A lower bound Many obligations formally addressed to member states or EU bodies reach companies too: directives that say "Member States shall ensure that entities…", duties to create enforcers with fining powers, Commission designations that switch obligations on, and supervisory fees. Our addressee coding is right 74% of the time; 30% of restrictions have no clear addressee.

Laws grow in negotiation

In 85 of 99 cases the act that came out of Parliament and Council had more restrictions than the Commission's proposal. The median act grew by 45% in words and 28% in restrictions. The largest increases: the AI Act (394 to 767), MiCA (766 to 1,117) and the European Health Data Space (398 to 609).

The second layer adds half again

Implementing and delegated acts set the technical detail: 690 of them, with 11,229 further restrictions. Guidance from the Commission and EU agencies is non-binding but shapes compliance.
