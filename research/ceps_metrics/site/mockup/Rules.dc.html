More than half of today's digital laws date from 2020 or later

Regulations, which apply directly in every member state, now outnumber directives almost two to one. Since 2020 the new acts have spread across almost every policy area, led by industrial policy, research and connectivity.

New acts carry more restrictions, but not denser ones

The average act adopted since 2025 contains about 270 restrictions, almost three times the average in the early 2000s, and above the 240 in the Sarbanes-Oxley Act. The acts are longer, not more tightly written: restrictions per 1,000 words have stayed between 12.5 and 15 for two decades.

Rules aimed directly at companies have quadrupled since 2018

About 13,300 restrictions name a private party, such as an undertaking, provider, platform or controller, as the one who must act: 7,106 in the main acts and 6,167 in their implementing and delegated acts. The average act adopted since 2020 binds companies directly 89 times, against 42 in the 2000s.

A lower bound Many obligations formally addressed to member states or EU bodies reach companies too: directives that say "Member States shall ensure that entities…", duties to create enforcers with fining powers, Commission designations that switch obligations on, and supervisory fees. Checked against a fresh hand-coded sample, our addressee coding is right 90% of the time, and every restriction now has an addressee. Counting duties on Member States whose content is company conduct (“Member States shall ensure that providers…”) would add about 450 more.

Laws grow in negotiation

In 84 of 99 cases the act that came out of Parliament and Council had more restrictions than the Commission's proposal. The median act grew by 45% in words and 27% in restrictions. The largest increases: the AI Act (392 to 764), MiCA (766 to 1,114) and the European Health Data Space (395 to 603).

The second layer adds half again

Implementing and delegated acts set the technical detail: 690 of them, with 10,840 further restrictions. Guidance from the Commission and EU agencies is non-binding but shapes compliance.

Restrictions by policy area

Trust and safety, finance and industrial policy hold the most restrictions, most of them added since 2015. Industrial policy and cybersecurity went from almost nothing to more than 1,700 restrictions each in under a decade. Restrictions binding private parties make up a larger share in trust and safety, finance and data protection than in industrial policy, where most rules bind member states and the EU.
