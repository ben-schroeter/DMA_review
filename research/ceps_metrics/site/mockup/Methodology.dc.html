Methodology

How we built the data

A plain-language guide to what we measured and how far to trust it. The technical documentation, code and validation samples are in the data download.

1. Scope

The 115 instruments in the CEPS inventory of EU digital law and policy (Zenner and Marcus, July 2025), plus eight enacted instruments listed in the same table. We measured the original act as published in the Official Journal; for instruments still pending in October 2026, the Commission proposal. English versions from EUR-Lex.

2. Counting restrictions

A restriction is a legal rule that requires, forbids or limits something. We count the markers that create them in the articles and annexes (recitals are not binding):

  • Obligations: shall, must, has to
  • Prohibitions: shall not, may not, is prohibited, "no … shall"
  • Restricted permissions: may only, may … only if
  • Duties of result: shall ensure
  • Explicit requirements: is required to, is obliged to
Counted: "Gatekeepers shall not process personal data of end users…"
Not counted: "This Regulation shall enter into force on…" (it regulates no one)

Excluded uses of "shall": entry into force, definitions, amendment instructions, saving clauses, rights and powers, and constitutive rules such as how a board is composed. Each restriction is assigned to who must act, using the grammatical subject: private parties, member states, EU institutions, or unclear.

How accurate is it? In a fresh hand-checked sample of 100 counted items, 84% were genuine restrictions, and the addressee was correct for 74% of those. Totals are reliable enough for comparisons of this size; addressee shares are approximate.

3. Secondary acts and guidance

Implementing and delegated acts are those EUR-Lex records as adopted under each instrument (770 items, measured like the main acts). Guidance comes from the Commission and from the EDPB, EBA and ESMA: final guidelines only, no Q&As or drafts. Amendments and national transposition measures come from EUR-Lex metadata.

4. Proposal vs adoption

For 99 adopted acts we compared the original Commission proposal with the adopted text, measured the same way on the legal text only. Three acts could not be compared because their proposals are not usable in EUR-Lex.

5. Reviewing the impact evidence

  • Promises: the objectives, indicators and quantified forecasts in each proposal's financial statement and impact assessment, each checked against the evidence available and given a status.
  • Sources: Commission evaluations, audits, statistics and research on effects on firms, prices, innovation, jobs and consumers. Initial impact assessments are shown with the promises; enforcement decisions and court rulings are not counted as evidence.
  • Depth: a full literature search for the 29 best-studied instruments; Commission evaluations plus a light search for other older acts; forecasts only for acts adopted since 2024.
  • Causal studies: those using a design that isolates the law's effect, such as difference-in-differences, event studies, regression discontinuity or synthetic control.
  • Checks: every source was located and its headline checked; each records whether the full text, an abstract or only a summary was read, and flags authors or funders with a stake.

6. Benchmarks and the US comparison

We counted restrictions in the Sarbanes-Oxley Act and in US counterparts of EU instruments with the same counter, adapted for US drafting. Precision on US text was 80% (68% for Sarbanes-Oxley). Ten pairs passed our fairness review. US words include text inserted into the US Code; EU words are articles and annexes.

7. Use of AI

Claude, an AI model, helped write the counting code, draft the objective summaries and run the literature search; every source and headline figure was checked against the original, and the validation samples were coded by hand. [To be completed with the project team's own review process.]

8. Limitations

  • Counts are not costs: one rule can be trivial, another expensive.
  • Obligations on member states in directives reach companies only after transposition.
  • "Not measured" means not found by our searches.
  • EUR-Lex links for very recent acts can lag.
