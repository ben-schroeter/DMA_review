Why it matters

Europe's growth problem is largely a technology problem

The EU's economy has fallen behind the United States over two decades. Around 70% of the gap in GDP per head comes from lower productivity, and the Draghi report on European competitiveness finds that gap "largely explained by the tech sector".

Regulation is one reason it gives: "around 100 tech-focused laws and over 270 regulators". Not everyone agrees it is the main cause; Anu Bradford points to fragmented markets and shallow capital markets instead. The debate is usually conducted without numbers. This site provides them.

The rulebook

More than half of today's digital laws date from 2020 or later

55 of the 105 adopted acts were passed since 2020. Together the 115 instruments run to about 2.8 million words.

In negotiation

Laws come out of Parliament and Council bigger than they went in

Comparing 99 Commission proposals with the acts finally adopted: 85 of them came out with more restrictions.

The second layer

Behind every law sits a second rulebook

Implementing and delegated acts fill in the detail. They add about 11,200 restrictions, close to half again on top of the main texts, plus guidance from the Commission and EU agencies, and tens of thousands of national transposition measures.

EU vs US

Matched against US laws doing the same job, EU acts carry about twice the rules

Ten pairs. The EU act has more restrictions in every one, 2.4 times as many at the median, and packs them more densely: 14.1 against 8.8 per 1,000 words.

EU digital acts against the Sarbanes-Oxley Act
