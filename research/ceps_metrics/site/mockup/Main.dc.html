Why it matters

Europe's growth problem is largely a technology problem

The EU's economy has fallen behind the United States over two decades. Around 70% of the gap in GDP per head comes from lower productivity, and the Draghi report on European competitiveness finds that gap "largely explained by the tech sector".

Regulation is one reason it gives: "around 100 tech-focused laws and over 270 regulators". Not everyone agrees it is the main cause; Anu Bradford points to fragmented markets and shallow capital markets instead. The debate is usually conducted without numbers. This site provides them.

The rulebook

More than half of today's digital laws date from 2020 or later

55 of the 105 adopted acts were passed since 2020, and they hold 65% of the 21,374 obligations in adopted texts. Together the 115 instruments run to about 2.8 million words.

Dashed lines mark when a new Commission took office (vdL: von der Leyen). Regulations apply directly in every member state; directives must be transposed into national law; decisions bind those they address. Policy areas follow the CEPS list (Zenner & Marcus, 2025). Impact

Do the rules work? Mostly, we don't know

We reviewed 489 studies, evaluations and think tank pieces, 68 of them causal, and checked what happened to every objective set out in the original proposals.
