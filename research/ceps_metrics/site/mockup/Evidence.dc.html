Do the rules work? For most, nobody has checked

For every instrument we collected the Commission's own forecasts, the indicators it said it would track, and every evaluation and study of its effects on companies, consumers, innovation and the economy we could find.

Most effects of most laws have never been measured

We sorted every study and forecast by the kind of effect it looks at. Of 840 pairs of law and effect, 312 have any evidence from after adoption and 54 have a causal study. For the five economic effects (costs, prices, competition, innovation and growth), 171 of 525 pairs have been looked at after adoption, 43 with a causal design. What gets checked most is whether a law was implemented, not what it did.

Each bar shows the laws in a policy area that are now in force: dark gold, at least one causal study on that effect; light gold, other evaluations, audits, statistics or studies; hatched, only the Commission's forecast; white, nothing found. Hover over a bar to see the laws. An empty cell means our searches found nothing, not that nothing exists. Counts are not quality, and one source can count for several effects. We sorted sources into effects by keywords on the outcomes recorded for each; a hand check of 150 found 86% tagged correctly before we fixed the commonest errors. Enforcement decisions and court rulings are not counted.

How we graded

StrongSeveral independent causal studies with consistent results, plus an official evaluation.
ModerateAt least one causal or robust quantitative study, or a solid evaluation with measured outcomes.
ThinDescriptive evidence, activity counts or qualitative reviews only.
Too early to tellAdopted, but too recently or not yet applying: only the impact assessment's forecasts, nothing yet to measure.
Not yet adoptedStill a proposal, or agreed but not formally adopted: only the impact assessment's forecasts.
What counts as evidenceEvaluations, audits, statistics, research and think tank pieces that assess what happened, each labelled by who produced it. Initial impact assessments are shown with the proposal's objectives; enforcement decisions and court rulings are not counted.
"Not measured"Not found by our searches. It is not proof that no study exists.

Case studies

Three laws show what the evidence can and cannot tell us.

One causal study, harm persists Unfair Commercial Practices Directive One causal study finds the directive raised consumer trust and cross-border shopping, but only where protection had been weak. Commission reviews find it cheap to comply with, yet online harm remains at least €7.9bn a year and almost all popular websites still use manipulative designs. Strong evidence, mostly of costs General Data Protection Regulation Independent causal studies consistently find costs: less venture funding, fewer new apps, higher data costs and more concentration among web vendors. The €2.3bn a year in administrative savings promised in the proposal has never been evaluated. Causal studies, no indicators Digital Markets Act Eight causal studies: choice screens shifted browser and search use a little; Google's redesigned results page sent more traffic to airlines and smaller comparison sites; rival map services saw no gain. None of the six indicators set in the proposal is reported.
