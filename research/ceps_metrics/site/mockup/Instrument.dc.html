Digital Markets Act

Regulation (EU) 2022/1925 ↗ Adopted 14 Sep 2022 In force 1 Nov 2022 Evidence: early and thin

What it does

Aims to ensure contestable and fair markets in the digital sector where large online platforms act as gatekeepers. It applies to core platform services, such as app stores, search engines and social networks, that designated gatekeepers offer to users in the EU. It sets out obligations and prohibitions for gatekeepers and empowers the Commission to designate them, investigate and enforce compliance.

What we know so far

The DMA has changed how the largest platforms behave: choice screens, steering, alternative app stores and less self-preferencing on Amazon. But none of the six promised indicators is reported systematically, and the Commission's €13bn consumer-benefit estimate has not been checked. Causal studies find large effects from browser choice screens, a one-third drop in Amazon's self-preferencing, no gain for rival map services, and, in one small study, lower start-up entry.

US counterpart

The closest US measure is the American Innovation and Choice Online Act, which would have barred dominant platforms from self-preferencing. It was never enacted; in the US, platform conduct is left to general antitrust law and the courts.
