Digital Services Act

Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 ↗Adopted 19 Oct 2022Evidence: moderate

Proposed 15 Dec 2020 and adopted 19 Oct 2022 under the first von der Leyen Commission (Margrethe Vestager, Thierry Breton) · Part of the Digital Decade agenda

What it does

Aims to create a safe, predictable and trusted online environment in which fundamental rights, including consumer protection, are protected. It applies to intermediary services offered in the EU, such as hosting services and online platforms. It sets conditions for the exemption of these providers from liability, imposes due diligence obligations scaled to the type and size of service, and sets up enforcement and cooperation between authorities.

What we know so far

The DSA set no monitoring indicators, and its forecast 1-1.8% boost to cross-border digital trade has not been evaluated. Enforcement has begun - X was fined EUR 120m in December 2025 - and the new transparency database has made moderation more visible, but audits of hundreds of millions of records show platforms report inconsistently. No study yet measures the DSA's effect on illegal content, users' safety or the platform economy.

Who it applies to

Size thresholds and exemptions: Micro and small platforms are exempt from the extra duties for online platforms and marketplaces and from transparency reports. The strictest rules apply only to very large platforms and search engines, with more than 45 million users in the EU.
