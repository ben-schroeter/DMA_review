# Codebook: rules and standards (Kaplow 1992)

Louis Kaplow, "Rules Versus Standards: An Economic Analysis", 42 Duke Law Journal 557 (1992). The distinction is **when the content
of the law is fixed**: a **rule** fixes it before people act; a **standard** leaves it to be decided afterwards, by a regulator or court.
It is not about how long or detailed the text is.

**Test for every item: can the addressee know, from this provision (read with the definitions and lists it points to), what
complies before acting, without a later decision by an authority or court about what the law required?**

## Codes

| Code | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| R | **Rule.** The required conduct is fixed in advance: an action, ban, number, deadline, closed list, format, procedure step, or a cross-reference to another fixed provision. | "Roaming providers shall charge roaming calls on a per second basis." "Providers of online platforms shall not present advertisements … based on profiling … using special categories of personal data." "ESMA shall submit the draft RTS by 30 June 2024." "The proposed acquirer shall submit annual financial statements including each of the following items:" "Personal data shall be processed in accordance with Regulation (EU) 2018/1725." |
| S | **Standard.** What complies turns on an open-ended evaluative term that someone must apply afterwards: appropriate, reasonable, adequate, sufficient, proportionate, effective (as a measure of the conduct), fair, good faith, harmful, without risk, as soon as possible, regularly, where relevant, take all necessary measures, ensure security, a risk-based approach, cooperate closely, coherence. | "Mobile network operators … shall negotiate in good faith." "Financial entities shall maintain redundant ICT capacities … adequate to ensure business needs." "The stopping action shall not cause deceleration harmful to the occupants." "The Commission shall regularly assess the efficiency …" "Member States shall ensure coherence with the existing frameworks …" |
| M | **Mixed.** A fixed duty or bright-line floor plus an open-ended element that is substantive, not just a qualifier. | "The testing … shall be performed, as appropriate, at any time throughout the development process, and, in any event, prior to their being placed on the market." "NRAs shall take appropriate measures ensuring that end-users are adequately informed … and are not switched … without their consent." |
| L | **Specified later.** The provision itself says its content will be set by a delegated or implementing act, regulatory/implementing technical standards, harmonised standards, common specifications or guidelines. Code the obligation that waits for the specification (not the duty of the Commission/ESA to adopt it, which is R). | "When specified in the delegated act adopted pursuant to Article 4, supply chain actors shall:" "the data carrier shall be physically present … as specified in the applicable delegated act" |
| D | **Left to member states.** The content is chosen by the member state when it transposes or implements (who decides, not when). | "Member States shall determine how such reference is to be made." "Member States shall provide for appropriate penalties …" "Each Member State shall lay down rules on whether and to what extent administrative fines may be imposed on public authorities." |
| N | **Not an obligation.** Constitutive or definitional statement, allocation of competence, list of bodies or tasks, programme objective without a duty, footnote, quoted case law, truncated fragment. | "The bodies of the Joint Undertaking shall be:" "Each supervisory authority shall be competent for …" |

## Decision rules

1. Judge the **core duty**. A loose qualifier on a fixed duty stays R: "notify … without undue delay", "publish … in an easily accessible manner", "an effective rebuttable presumption", "a brief summary".
2. A duty triggered by **the addressee's own judgement** with a fixed consequence is R ("Where the Commission considers that the emergency no longer exists, it shall propose deactivation"). A trigger that a court reviews against an open-ended test is S ("clear and demonstrable grounds to suspect").
3. **Essential requirements** for products ("designed so that … without putting persons at risk", "fit for their function") are S, even when long and technical, unless they fix a value or a specific feature (then R: "bears a type, batch or serial number").
4. Duties to **consider / take into account** listed factors in an authority's assessment are S; duties to **include / contain / submit / publish** listed items are R.
5. Duties to **evaluate / report on / examine** a named subject are R when the subject and timing are fixed; "regularly assess", "monitor" without fixed content or timing are S.
6. A **chapeau with a list** ("shall include the following:") is R if the list items are fixed.
7. If both a fixed element and a substantive open-ended element matter, use M; do not overuse M.
