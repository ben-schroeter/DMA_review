ePrivacy Directive

Directive 2002/58/EC ↗Adopted 12 Jul 2002Evidence: moderate

Proposed 12 Jul 2000 and adopted 12 Jul 2002 under the Prodi Commission (Erkki Liikanen) · Part of the Lisbon agenda

What it does

Aims to ensure an equivalent level of protection of privacy and personal data in electronic communications across the EU, complementing general data protection rules. It requires providers to keep communications confidential and secure, restricts the use of traffic and location data, requires consent for storing information such as cookies on users' devices, and regulates unsolicited marketing communications.

What we know so far

The ePrivacy Directive set no measurable targets. The Commission's own 2017 evaluation found its cookie-consent rule had not given users real control and was over-inclusive, at around EUR 658 compliance cost per business. Independent research found the EU tracking rules made online display advertising markedly less effective, especially for news sites. The proposed ePrivacy Regulation meant to fix this was withdrawn in 2025.
