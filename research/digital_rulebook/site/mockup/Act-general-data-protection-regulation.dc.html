Legal instruments / Data & Privacy

General Data Protection Regulation

Regulation (EU) 2016/679 ↗Adopted 27 Apr 2016Evidence: strong

Proposed 25 Jan 2012 under the second Barroso Commission (Neelie Kroes) and adopted 27 Apr 2016 under the Juncker Commission (Andrus Ansip) · Part of the Europe 2020 agenda

What it does

Lays down rules to protect people with regard to the processing of personal data and to ensure the free movement of such data within the EU. It applies to controllers and processors, including those outside the EU targeting people in the EU. It sets processing principles and legal bases, grants individuals rights such as access and erasure, and creates independent supervisory authorities with corrective powers.

What we know so far

Independent causal studies consistently find costs: less venture funding, fewer apps (a third exited, entry halved), higher data costs (+22%), lower recorded web traffic (-12%) and more concentration among web vendors favouring Google and Facebook. The Commission's reviews report rising enforcement (EUR 4.2bn in fines) and better-resourced regulators, but the promised EUR 2.3bn a year in administrative savings has never been evaluated.

Who it applies to

Size thresholds and exemptions: Organisations with fewer than 250 employees need not keep records of processing activities, unless the processing is risky, not occasional or involves sensitive or criminal-record data (Article 30(5)).

Objectives and key results

Objectives are the aims the Commission set for the law. Key results are how it said it would measure them: indicators in the proposal and forecasts in the impact assessment, checked against the evidence available in October 2026.
