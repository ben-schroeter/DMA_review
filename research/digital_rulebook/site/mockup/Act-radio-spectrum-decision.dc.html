Radio Spectrum Decision

Decision 676/2002/EC ↗Adopted 7 Mar 2002Evidence: thin

Proposed 12 Jul 2000 and adopted 7 Mar 2002 under the Prodi Commission (Erkki Liikanen) · Part of the Lisbon agenda

What it does

Establishes a policy and legal framework to coordinate radio spectrum use in the EU and, where appropriate, harmonise conditions for its availability and efficient use. It sets procedures for strategic spectrum planning, creates a Radio Spectrum Committee to help the Commission adopt harmonisation measures, and requires information on spectrum use to be made publicly available.

What we know so far

The 2002 decision set up the EU process for harmonising technical rules on radio spectrum. The Commission credits it with making more mobile broadband spectrum available than other world regions, but countries were slow to put that spectrum to use: by 2015 they had assigned only about 708 MHz of a 1,200 MHz target, and 5G bands took over a decade to assign. No independent study isolates its economic effects.
