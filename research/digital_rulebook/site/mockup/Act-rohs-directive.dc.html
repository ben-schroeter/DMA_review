RoHS Directive

Directive 2011/65/EU ↗Adopted 8 Jun 2011Evidence: thin

Proposed 3 Dec 2008 under the first Barroso Commission (Viviane Reding) and adopted 8 Jun 2011 under the second Barroso Commission (Neelie Kroes) · Part of the Lisbon agenda

What it does

Restricts the use of hazardous substances (such as lead, mercury, cadmium, hexavalent chromium and certain flame retardants and phthalates) in electrical and electronic equipment, to protect human health and the environment and support recovery of waste equipment. Manufacturers must ensure compliance, draw up technical documentation and a declaration of conformity and affix the CE marking; exemptions for specific uses are granted and reviewed through delegated acts.

What we know so far

The Commission's 2023 evaluation found that the Directive has reduced hazardous substances in electrical and electronic equipment and works well overall, including as a global benchmark, but that the processes for exemptions and new restrictions lack transparency and efficiency; it proposed moving the technical assessment to the European Chemicals Agency.
