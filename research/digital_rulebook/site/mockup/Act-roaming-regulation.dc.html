Roaming Regulation

Regulation (EU) 2022/612 ↗Adopted 6 Apr 2022Evidence: strong

Proposed 24 Feb 2021 and adopted 6 Apr 2022 under the first von der Leyen Commission (Margrethe Vestager, Thierry Breton) · Part of the Digital Decade agenda

What it does

Aims to ensure that mobile users travelling within the EU do not pay excessive prices for calls, SMS and data compared with domestic prices. It sets maximum charges that network operators may levy on each other at wholesale level and the conditions for wholesale access to mobile networks, and governs retail roaming charges. It also requires roaming providers to give users clearer information on charges, including outside the EU.

What we know so far

Since roaming charges in the EU ended in 2017, mobile data use abroad has risen about 48-fold, and peer-reviewed studies find large consumer gains (about EUR 2.44 per traveller per day) that outweigh operators' lost profits, with no sign of higher domestic prices. The 2022 recast kept the regime sustainable: no network operator has needed a surcharge derogation since 2022. The main shortfall is quality: a 2025 study found 76% of travellers get slower download speeds abroad than at home.
