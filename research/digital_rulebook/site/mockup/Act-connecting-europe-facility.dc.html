Connecting Europe Facility

Regulation (EU) 2021/1153 ↗Adopted 7 Jul 2021Evidence: thin

Proposed 6 Jun 2018 under the Juncker Commission (Andrus Ansip) and adopted 7 Jul 2021 under the first von der Leyen Commission (Margrethe Vestager, Thierry Breton) · Part of the Digital Single Market agenda

What it does

Funds the building, development and modernisation of trans-European networks in transport, energy and digital sectors for 2021-2027, and facilitates cross-border cooperation on renewable energy. In the digital sector it supports projects such as very high capacity backbone networks, 5G systems along transport corridors and the digitalisation of transport and energy networks. Its financial envelope is EUR 33.71 billion.

What we know so far

The Connecting Europe Facility co-funds cross-border transport, energy and digital networks. By 2025 the transport money was fully committed and calls had been nearly three times oversubscribed, but only a handful of the 600+ funded transport projects were finished. There is no measured evidence yet of effects on trade, energy markets or growth, and the formal interim evaluation was still under way.

Objectives and key results

Objectives are the aims the Commission set for the law. Key results are how it said it would measure them: indicators in the proposal and forecasts in the impact assessment, checked against the evidence available in October 2026.

Objectives
  • Transport: complete TEN-T core network
  • Energy: integrate internal energy market (PCIs, cross-border renewables)
  • Digital: 5G corridors and backbone networks
  • Growth, cohesion, decarbonisation
