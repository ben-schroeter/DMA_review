Unfair Contract Terms Directive

Directive 93/13/EEC ↗Adopted 5 Apr 1993Evidence: thin

Proposed 24 Jul 1990 and adopted 5 Apr 1993 under the Delors Commission (Martin Bangemann) · Part of the Market opening agenda

What it does

Aims to protect consumers from unfair terms in contracts with sellers and suppliers by approximating national laws. It applies to contract terms that have not been individually negotiated, provides that unfair terms do not bind the consumer, requires written terms to be in plain, intelligible language, and requires Member States to provide means to stop the continued use of unfair terms.

What we know so far

The 1993 directive makes unfair standard contract terms non-binding on consumers. Commission reviews found it largely fit for purpose and cheap to comply with, and court actions have forced companies to change terms, but legal studies and the 2024 review show many online terms still look unfair and enforcement is weak. No study measures its effect on prices, trade or consumer losses.

No impact assessment: proposed before the Commission introduced them in 2003
