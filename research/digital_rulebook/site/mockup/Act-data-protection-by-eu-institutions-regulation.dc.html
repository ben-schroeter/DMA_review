Data protection by EU institutions Regulation

Regulation (EU) 2018/1725 ↗Adopted 23 Oct 2018Evidence: thin

Proposed 10 Jan 2017 and adopted 23 Oct 2018 under the Juncker Commission (Andrus Ansip) · Part of the Digital Single Market agenda

What it does

Sets rules protecting individuals when EU institutions, bodies, offices and agencies process their personal data, and on the free movement of such data. It aligns these rules with the General Data Protection Regulation, sets out data subjects' rights and controllers' obligations, and gives the European Data Protection Supervisor the task of monitoring compliance.

What we know so far

These rules bring data protection in the EU institutions in line with the GDPR. The Commission's 2022 review, based on a survey of 56 EU bodies, judged them to be working well: over half gave their data protection officers more resources and routine checks by the supervisor fell as intended, but the supervisor had not issued any fines. No independent study has assessed the rules' effects.

Objectives and key results

Objectives are the aims the Commission set for the law. Key results are how it said it would measure them: indicators in the proposal and forecasts in the impact assessment, checked against the evidence available in October 2026.

Objectives
  • Ensure consistent application of data protection rules throughout the Union (alignment of EU institutions' rules with the GDPR)
  • Rationalise the governance model and strengthen compliance and enforcement in EU institutions and bodies
