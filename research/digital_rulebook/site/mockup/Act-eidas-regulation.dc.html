eIDAS Regulation

Regulation (EU) 910/2014 ↗Adopted 23 Jul 2014Evidence: thin

Proposed 4 Jun 2012 and adopted 23 Jul 2014 under the second Barroso Commission (Neelie Kroes) · Part of the Europe 2020 agenda

What it does

Aims to enhance trust in electronic transactions in the internal market. It requires Member States to recognise notified electronic identification means of other Member States for access to online public services, lays down rules for trust service providers, and establishes a legal framework for electronic signatures, seals, time stamps, documents, registered delivery services and website authentication certificates.

What we know so far

eIDAS aimed to let people use their national e-ID and e-signatures across the EU. The Commission's 2021 evaluation found trust services such as qualified e-signatures took off (202 qualified providers), but cross-border e-ID use was tiny - at most tens of thousands of logins a year per country - so costs exceeded benefits. Notification has since spread to 24 of 27 countries, and the 2024 revision (European Digital Identity Wallet) is meant to fix low uptake; no independent study measures economic effects.
