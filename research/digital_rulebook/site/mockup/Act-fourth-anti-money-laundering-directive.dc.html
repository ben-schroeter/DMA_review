Fourth Anti-Money Laundering Directive

Directive (EU) 2015/849 ↗Adopted 20 May 2015Evidence: thin

Proposed 5 Feb 2013 under the second Barroso Commission (Neelie Kroes) and adopted 20 May 2015 under the Juncker Commission (Andrus Ansip) · Part of the Europe 2020 agenda

In force until 10 July 2027, when the Anti-Money Laundering Regulation (2024/1624) replaces it. Counted as originally adopted; the 2018 amendment (Directive 2018/843) is not included in the count.

What it does

Aims to prevent the use of the EU financial system for money laundering and terrorist financing. Obliged entities (banks, financial institutions, auditors, lawyers, estate agents, gambling providers, traders in goods above set cash thresholds and, after the 2018 amendment, crypto-exchange and wallet providers) must apply risk-based customer due diligence, identify beneficial owners, report suspicious transactions to financial intelligence units and keep records; Member States must set up central beneficial ownership registers. Applies until 10 July 2027, when the Anti-Money Laundering Regulation replaces it.

What we know so far

The Commission's 2019 assessment found major divergences in how Member States apply the anti-money laundering rules, a structural weakness that led to the 2024 package replacing this Directive with a directly applicable Regulation from July 2027. The European Court of Auditors (2021) found EU action in the banking sector fragmented and implementation insufficient.
