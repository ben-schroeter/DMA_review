Digital Euro Regulation

2023/0212(COD) ↗ProposalEvidence: too early to tell

Proposed 28 Jun 2023 by the first von der Leyen Commission (Margrethe Vestager, Thierry Breton); still pending in October 2026 · Part of the Digital Decade agenda

What it does

Would establish the digital euro, a digital form of the single currency issued by the European Central Bank, to adapt the euro to technological change. It would give it legal tender status, set rules for its distribution by payment service providers, online and offline use, holding limits, privacy and data protection, and require basic digital euro services to be provided free of charge to individuals.

What we know so far

The Commission judged that a digital euro's benefits outweigh set-up costs of several billion euros if holding limits protect banks. ECB simulations suggest a EUR 3,000 limit would leave bank funding largely unaffected in normal times, though a severe crisis could shift up to EUR 699 billion of deposits; a bank-funded study puts banks' costs at EUR 18 billion or more, well above ECB estimates. The law is still being negotiated, so there is no evidence of actual effects.
