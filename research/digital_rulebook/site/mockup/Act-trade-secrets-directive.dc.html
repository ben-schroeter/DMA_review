Trade Secrets Directive

Directive (EU) 2016/943 ↗Adopted 8 Jun 2016Evidence: thin

Proposed 28 Nov 2013 under the second Barroso Commission (Neelie Kroes) and adopted 8 Jun 2016 under the Juncker Commission (Andrus Ansip) · Part of the Europe 2020 agenda

What it does

Lays down rules to protect undisclosed know-how and business information against unlawful acquisition, use and disclosure. It sets a common definition of a trade secret, specifies lawful and unlawful conduct, and provides exceptions, including for whistleblowing and freedom of expression. It requires Member States to provide civil remedies such as injunctions and damages and to preserve confidentiality in court proceedings.

What we know so far

The Trade Secrets Directive aimed to encourage innovation by giving firms the same protection for confidential know-how across the EU; the Commission cited a survey in which 40% of firms said they held back from sharing secrets. An EU-commissioned study of about 700 court cases shows litigation still varies widely between countries and rarely crosses borders. No study has measured whether the directive changed innovation, knowledge-sharing or worker mobility.
