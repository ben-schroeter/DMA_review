Payment Services Directive 2 (PSD2)

Directive (EU) 2015/2366 ↗Adopted 25 Nov 2015Evidence: moderate

Proposed 24 Jul 2013 under the second Barroso Commission (Neelie Kroes) and adopted 25 Nov 2015 under the Juncker Commission (Andrus Ansip) · Part of the Europe 2020 agenda

What it does

Aims to develop an integrated, secure and efficient market for electronic payments in the EU. It sets categories and authorisation rules for payment service providers, including new payment initiation and account information services, and rules on transparency and the rights and obligations of users and providers. It requires strong customer authentication and gives authorised third parties access to payment accounts.

What we know so far

PSD2's strong customer authentication cut card fraud sharply (70-80% lower on authenticated remote payments) and open-banking rules helped new payment firms enter; cross-country research links open banking to more fintech investment. But open-banking uptake is uneven, banks report high API costs, card markets stay national and the promised fall in payment costs for merchants has not materialised. Industry data point to some lost online sales from authentication friction.

Objectives and key results

Objectives are the aims the Commission set for the law. Key results are how it said it would measure them: indicators in the proposal and forecasts in the impact assessment, checked against the evidence available in October 2026.

Objectives
  • Develop an EU-wide market for electronic payments
  • Address standardisation and interoperability gaps for card, internet and mobile payments
  • Eliminate hurdles for competition, in particular for card and internet payments
  • Align charging and steering practices across the EU
  • Cover emerging payment services and instruments
  • Ensure consistent application of licensing and supervision across Member States
  • Ensure adequate and consistent consumer protection
