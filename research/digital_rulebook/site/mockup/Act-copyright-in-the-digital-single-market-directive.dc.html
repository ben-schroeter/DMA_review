Copyright in the Digital Single Market Directive

Directive (EU) 2019/790 ↗Adopted 17 Apr 2019Evidence: moderate

Proposed 14 Sep 2016 and adopted 17 Apr 2019 under the Juncker Commission (Andrus Ansip) · Part of the Digital Single Market agenda

What it does

Further harmonises EU copyright law to take account of digital and cross-border uses of protected content. It creates exceptions for text and data mining, teaching and cultural heritage preservation, eases licensing of out-of-commerce works, introduces a right for press publishers, sets liability rules for online content-sharing platforms, and requires transparency and fair remuneration for authors and performers.

What we know so far

The Commission promised that the Directive would help news publishers and other right holders license their content to online platforms, but set no measurable targets and has not yet evaluated it. Studies of earlier national publishers' rights found they raised little money and that when Google News left Spain, traffic to news sites fell by 8-14%. In France, Google paid only after EUR 750m in competition fines; the effect of the upload-filter rules (Art. 17) on creators, platforms and users has not been measured.

Objectives and key results

Objectives are the aims the Commission set for the law. Key results are how it said it would measure them: indicators in the proposal and forecasts in the impact assessment, checked against the evidence available in October 2026.

Objectives
  • Press publishers' right (Art. 15)
  • Use of content by upload platforms (Art. 17)
  • Exceptions (TDM, teaching, preservation)
  • Transparency and remuneration for creators
