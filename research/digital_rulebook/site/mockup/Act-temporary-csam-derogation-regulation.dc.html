Temporary CSAM Derogation Regulation

Regulation (EU) 2021/1232 ↗Adopted 14 Jul 2021Evidence: thin

Proposed 10 Sep 2020 and adopted 14 Jul 2021 under the first von der Leyen Commission (Margrethe Vestager, Thierry Breton) · Part of the Digital Decade agenda

What it does

Lays down temporary, strictly limited derogations from ePrivacy Directive confidentiality rules, solely to allow providers of number-independent interpersonal communications services, such as messaging and webmail, to voluntarily use specific technologies to detect, report and remove online child sexual abuse material. It sets conditions on such processing, including data protection safeguards, human oversight, redress and reporting obligations.

What we know so far

This temporary law lets messaging services keep voluntarily scanning for child sexual abuse material despite EU privacy rules. Two Commission reports (2023, 2025) say national and platform data are too incomplete to measure its effect reliably, although thousands of children were identified and millions of images removed. Data protection authorities warn safeguards against blanket scanning remain weak.

No impact assessment · none (Commission judged an IA not appropriate)
