A regulation on fair business-to-consumer practices that replaces the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive, so one directly applicable act replaces 27 national transpositions, and adds rules for practices the current law handles poorly online.

Impact assessment SWD(2026) 1101

Latest · November 2026

The Commission proposed the Digital Fairness Act on 11 November 2026, in a consumer package with a directive simplifying the Consumer Rights Directive and the revision of the Consumer Protection Cooperation Regulation. The proposals are backed by an impact assessment, on which the Regulatory Scrutiny Board gave a positive opinion with reservations on 3 July 2026. They now go to the European Parliament and the Council; no Parliament committee has been named yet.

What it would do

  • Bans manipulative design (dark patterns), such as false claims that a product is almost sold out.
  • Clear pricing for virtual in-app currencies and for dynamic pricing of cultural and sports events, including the highest possible price in virtual queues.
  • Fairness by design: consumers can switch off addictive features; gambling-like features such as pay-to-win are banned for minors.
  • Influencers must disclose commercial content; personalised ads and prices that target minors or use sensitive data are restricted.
  • Easier cancellation of digital contracts and subscriptions, an end to unwanted automatic renewals and free-trial traps, a right to reach a human in customer service, and rules for purchases made by AI agents.

What it would change in the Digital Acquis

  • Unfair Commercial Practices Directive · repealed and replaced by the regulation
  • Consumer Rights Directive · amended by the companion simplification directive
  • ePrivacy Directive · amended: business-to-consumer communications covered by consumer law instead
  • Consumer Protection Cooperation Regulation · replaced by the companion enforcement proposal
  • Digital Services Act · overlaps on dark patterns
