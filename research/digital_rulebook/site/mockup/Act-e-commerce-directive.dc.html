E-commerce Directive

Directive 2000/31/EC ↗Adopted 8 Jun 2000Evidence: thin

Proposed 18 Nov 1998 under the Santer Commission (Martin Bangemann) and adopted 8 Jun 2000 under the Prodi Commission (Erkki Liikanen) · Part of the Market opening agenda

What it does

Aims to ensure the free movement of information society services between Member States. It subjects online service providers to the rules of the Member State where they are established, sets information requirements for providers and commercial communications, enables contracts to be concluded electronically, and limits the liability of intermediaries that host, cache or transmit third-party content.

What we know so far

The E-commerce Directive set no measurable targets. The Commission's 2020 evaluation concluded that its core rules, including liability protection for online intermediaries, helped digital services grow across borders, but national courts applied them inconsistently; this led to the Digital Services Act. No independent study has measured the safe harbours' effect on investment or innovation in Europe; the best-known estimate is a Google-funded simulation.
