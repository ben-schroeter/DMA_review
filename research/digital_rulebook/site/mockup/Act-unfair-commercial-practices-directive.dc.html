Unfair Commercial Practices Directive

Directive 2005/29/EC ↗Adopted 11 May 2005Evidence: moderate

Proposed 18 Jun 2003 under the Prodi Commission (Erkki Liikanen) and adopted 11 May 2005 under the first Barroso Commission (Viviane Reding) · Part of the Lisbon agenda

What it does

Aims to achieve a high level of consumer protection and a properly functioning internal market by approximating national rules on unfair business-to-consumer commercial practices. It sets a general ban on unfair practices, specifically prohibits misleading and aggressive practices, and lists practices considered unfair in all circumstances. Member States must provide effective means to enforce compliance.

What we know so far

The UCPD replaced national rules on misleading and aggressive selling in 2005-2007. One causal study finds it raised consumer trust and cross-border shopping, but only in countries with weak protection beforehand. The Commission's 2017 and 2024 reviews found the rules cheap to comply with (about EUR 0.5-0.7 billion a year for the three core consumer laws), but online harm remains large - at least EUR 7.9 billion a year - and almost all popular websites still use manipulative designs.
