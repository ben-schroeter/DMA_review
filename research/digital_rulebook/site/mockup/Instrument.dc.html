Digital Markets Act

Regulation (EU) 2022/1925 ↗Adopted 14 Sep 2022Evidence: moderate

Proposed 15 Dec 2020 and adopted 14 Sep 2022 under the first von der Leyen Commission (Margrethe Vestager, Thierry Breton) · Part of the Digital Decade agenda

What it does

Aims to ensure contestable and fair markets in the digital sector where large online platforms act as gatekeepers. It applies to core platform services, such as app stores, search engines and social networks, that designated gatekeepers offer to users in the EU. It sets out obligations and prohibitions for gatekeepers and empowers the Commission to designate them, investigate and enforce compliance.

What we know so far

The DMA has changed gatekeeper conduct (choice screens, steering, alternative app stores, consent, less self-preferencing on Amazon) and produced EUR 1.59bn of fines, but none of the six promised indicators is reported systematically and the EUR 13bn consumer-surplus estimate has not been assessed. Causal studies find large default effects for browsers (Firefox +91% on iOS), a one-third drop in Amazon's self-preferencing after designation, but no shift to rival map services; one small study finds lower start-up entry and investment. Theory (Madio, Manenti & Motta 2026) supports the DMA's equal-access design.

Who it applies to

Size thresholds and exemptions: Only designated gatekeepers are bound: firms with at least EUR 7.5 billion annual EU turnover or EUR 75 billion market value, running a platform service with at least 45 million monthly users in the EU.

Objectives and key results

Objectives are the aims the Commission set for the law. Key results are how it said it would measure them: indicators in the proposal and forecasts in the impact assessment, checked against the evidence available in October 2026.

Objectives

  • Address gatekeepers' unfair conduct
  • Enhance coherence and legal certainty to preserve the internal market
