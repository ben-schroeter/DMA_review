Radio Equipment Directive

Directive 2014/53/EU ↗Adopted 16 Apr 2014Evidence: thin

Proposed 17 Oct 2012 and adopted 16 Apr 2014 under the second Barroso Commission (Neelie Kroes) · Part of the Europe 2020 agenda

What it does

Establishes the framework for placing radio equipment on the EU market and putting it into service. It requires equipment such as phones and connected devices to meet essential requirements on health and safety, electromagnetic compatibility and efficient use of radio spectrum, and allows further requirements, for example on common chargers or cybersecurity, backed by conformity assessment and CE marking.

What we know so far

The Radio Equipment Directive promised higher product compliance with little extra burden. Market surveillance data show around 60% of inspected products failing in 2022, but because inspectors target risky products this does not show whether compliance has improved since the 28-56% baseline. The promised indicators on red tape and innovation were never measured, and no independent study was found.
