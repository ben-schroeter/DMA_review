Database Directive

Directive 96/9/EC ↗Adopted 11 Mar 1996Evidence: thin

Proposed 13 May 1992 under the Delors Commission (Martin Bangemann) and adopted 11 Mar 1996 under the Santer Commission (Martin Bangemann) · Part of the Market opening agenda

What it does

Aims to harmonise the legal protection of databases in any form across the internal market. It protects databases that reflect their author's own intellectual creation by copyright, and creates a separate sui generis right allowing makers who have made a substantial investment to prevent extraction or re-use of the whole or a substantial part of the contents, subject to exceptions.

What we know so far

The Database Directive created a special 'sui generis' right to encourage investment in European databases. Two Commission evaluations, in 2005 and 2018, found no proven effect on database production or the industry's competitiveness: EU database entries in 2004 were back at their 1998 level (about 3,100) while the US, which has no such right, pulled further ahead. The Commission kept the right because its costs appear modest and publishers value it.
