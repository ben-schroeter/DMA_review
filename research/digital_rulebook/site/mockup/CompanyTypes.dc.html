What the rules mean for
different kinds of company

EU digital law does not fall evenly. We grouped companies into six types by what they do, and counted the obligations that apply to each type on top of the rules every company follows.

Six types of company

One company can be several types. Amazon, for example, is a gatekeeper and a very large online marketplace, one of the largest cloud providers through AWS, and a maker of connected devices; each layer brings its own rules.

Rules for platforms, cloud and AI barely existed before 2018

Makers of connected products and financial firms carry the most type-specific obligations, about 3,801 and 3,747, but both already had sizeable rulebooks in 2018. For platforms, cloud and AI the rules are almost entirely new: gatekeepers and very large platforms went from 21 specific obligations at the start of 2018 to 1,452, and cloud, data and AI providers from none to 1,363. Every company also follows a baseline of 2,059 obligations, from data protection to consumer, tax and company law.

How often and how fast companies must report

About one in seven obligations on companies, 15%, is a duty to report, notify, disclose or keep records. Most reporting duties are triggered by events, not by the calendar. Makers of connected products have 70 duties to report something without undue delay or within 72 hours, such as actively exploited vulnerabilities under the Cyber Resilience Act; financial firms have 53 and gatekeepers 40. Every company has 46 more under the baseline, many of them under data protection law. Periodic reporting is a small share for every type. For many duties the provision itself does not say when: the timing is set elsewhere in the act, in implementing acts or by the authority.

Small firms get little relief: a micro firm faces 93% of the rules a large one does

Few EU digital laws scale their obligations to the size of the firm. Of 12,466 obligations on companies, only 846 do not apply to a firm with fewer than 10 staff. Most of that relief comes from three laws: NIS2 cybersecurity duties start at medium size, the Digital Services Act spares micro and small platforms its platform and marketplace duties, and the Data Act spares micro and small makers its data-sharing chapter. Finance and telecoms get almost none, and nothing changes between 250 and 750 staff. The one big step is at the top: gatekeepers and very large platforms take on about 300 more obligations under the Digital Markets Act and the Digital Services Act.

Most relief for small firms leaves the obligation in place

Size-based relief comes in three forms. A few broad exemptions switch whole chapters off: NIS2, the platform sections of the Digital Services Act and the Data Act's data-sharing chapter remove 476 obligations for the smallest firms. More numerous clauses only lighten an obligation, with simpler documentation or less frequent updates, and reach 111. Lower fines for SMEs in five laws, including the AI Act and the Cyber Resilience Act, cover 587 more but change nothing about what a firm must do.

How we assigned the rules. For each of the 100 adopted laws we coded which types it applies to, from its scope provisions: in full, or only in part. Where a law has tiers, we used its articles: in the Digital Services Act, for example, Articles 11 to 15 apply to all intermediary services, 16 to 18 to hosting services, 19 to 32 to online platforms and marketplaces, and 33 to 43 to very large platforms only. Elsewhere we counted the obligations whose wording names that kind of company. The baseline covers data protection, consumer law, VAT, company law, competition and intellectual property. Laws addressed to public bodies and EU funding programmes, whose company obligations are grant conditions, are not counted for any type; nor are trust service providers under eIDAS, a specialised group outside the six types.

These are profiles of types of company, not legal assessments of named firms: what applies to a given firm depends on its activities, size and designations. Company names are illustrations; gatekeeper and very-large-platform status follows the Commission's designations. The full table of laws by type is in the data download.
