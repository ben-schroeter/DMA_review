EVIDENCE-BASED POLICYMAKING FOR THE DIGITAL ECONOMY

How much does Europe regulate tech, and does it work?

Europe is a self-proclaimed regulatory superpower for the digital economy, yet little is known about the scale and effects of its tech regulation on markets, firms, and the broader economy. This project tracks and quantifies EU tech laws, and maps corresponding empirical research to help European policymakers make better, evidence-based choices.

Why it matters

Europe's growth problem
is largely a technology problem

Over the last three decades, economic growth in Europe has fallen behind the United States. Europe’s households have paid the price in foregone living standards. On a per capita basis, real disposable income has grown almost twice as much in the US as in the EU since 2000. Most of this gap in GDP per capita comes from lower productivity, and the Draghi report on European competitiveness finds that gap "largely explained by the tech sector".

Regulation is one of the contributing factors to Europe's malaise. As Draghi notes: “innovative companies that want to scale up in Europe are hindered at every stage by inconsistent and restrictive regulations.” At the same time, regulations also build consumer trust, support effective competition, and protect human rights. Getting regulation right is therefore fundamental to solving Europe's growth challenges.

The rulebook

More than half of today's digital laws date from 2020 or later

The digital economy took off from the mid-1990s, enabled by key pieces of regulation like the e-Commerce Directive (2000). However, 41 of the 100 adopted legislative acts impacting the digital economy were passed only since 2020, and they account for 57% of the 18,430 obligations in adopted texts. Together the 100 laws run to about 2.2 million words, forming the densest regulatory environment for digital technologies in the world. Recently, the Vice-President of the Court of Justice of the EU, Thomas von Danwitz, stated that tech legislation was “on the brink of being un-understandable.”

Impact

Do the rules work? Mostly, we don't know

The EU Digital Acquis has become complex but we don't know if it works. There are no systematic ex-post evaluations and the original legislative objectives are mostly not tracked.  Our database contains 441 studies, evaluations, and think tank pieces – 66 of them using causal empirical methods. For most pieces of legislation the evidence base is rather thin.
