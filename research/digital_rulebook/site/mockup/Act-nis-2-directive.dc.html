Legal instruments / Cybersecurity

NIS 2 Directive

Directive (EU) 2022/2555 ↗Adopted 14 Dec 2022Evidence: thin

Proposed 16 Dec 2020 and adopted 14 Dec 2022 under the first von der Leyen Commission (Margrethe Vestager, Thierry Breton) · Part of the Digital Decade agenda

What it does

Aims to achieve a high common level of cybersecurity across the EU. It requires Member States to adopt national cybersecurity strategies and designate competent authorities, crisis management bodies and incident response teams (CSIRTs). Medium-sized and large entities in sectors listed in its annexes, such as energy, transport, health and digital infrastructure, must take cybersecurity risk-management measures and report significant incidents.

What we know so far

The Commission expected NIS 2 to cut the cost of cyber incidents by EUR 11.3 billion over ten years, at the price of up to 22% more security spending for newly covered firms. Neither figure has been measured: ENISA's surveys show security budgets steady at about 9% of IT spending, compliance as the main driver of investment, and newly covered sectors lagging behind those already regulated. Transposition was slow - four countries were taken to court in July 2026 - and no study yet measures whether incidents have fallen.

Objectives and key results

Objectives are the aims the Commission set for the law. Key results are how it said it would measure them: indicators in the proposal and forecasts in the impact assessment, checked against the evidence available in October 2026.

Objectives
  • Raise cyber resilience across all relevant sectors
  • Compliance cost for companies
  • Fewer and cheaper incidents
  • National authorities
  • Consistent implementation across Member States
