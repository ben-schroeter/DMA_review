FDI Screening Regulation

Regulation (EU) 2019/452 ↗Adopted 19 Mar 2019Evidence: moderate

Proposed 13 Sep 2017 and adopted 19 Mar 2019 under the Juncker Commission (Andrus Ansip) · Part of the Digital Single Market agenda

What it does

Establishes an EU framework for Member States' screening of foreign direct investments on grounds of security or public order. It sets minimum requirements for national screening mechanisms, creates a cooperation mechanism through which Member States and the Commission share information and comments on investments, and allows the Commission to issue opinions. Each Member State keeps the final decision on whether to screen an investment.

What we know so far

The FDI Screening Regulation set no measurable targets beyond a review. By 2024, 24 of 27 countries screened foreign investments and handled over 3,000 cases, blocking about 1% of those formally screened; the Commission's 2024 evaluation says the system protects security without deterring investment, while EU auditors found 'blind spots'. Independent studies suggest screening reduces foreign takeovers in newly screened sectors by roughly 12-16%, so the claim of no chilling effect is contested; a revised Regulation was adopted in 2026.
