Toys Safety Directive

Directive 2009/48/EC ↗Adopted 18 Jun 2009Evidence: thin

Proposed 25 Jan 2008 and adopted 18 Jun 2009 under the first Barroso Commission (Viviane Reding) · Part of the Lisbon agenda

What it does

Lays down rules on the safety of toys and their free movement in the EU. It applies to products designed or intended for play by children under 14. It sets essential safety requirements, including limits on chemicals, and assigns obligations to manufacturers, importers and distributors, including conformity assessment, CE marking, warnings and technical documentation, backed by market surveillance.

What we know so far

The 2009 directive tightened safety and chemical rules for toys sold in the EU. The Commission's 2020 evaluation found intra-EU toy trade grew about 60% and compliance costs were modest (about 2% of turnover once), but one in three inspected toys broke the rules and there are no data on whether child injuries fell. A replacement regulation is forecast to avoid EUR 240 million to 1.2 billion a year in health damage.
