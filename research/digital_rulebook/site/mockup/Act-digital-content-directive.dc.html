Digital Content Directive

Directive (EU) 2019/770 ↗Adopted 20 May 2019Evidence: thin

Proposed 9 Dec 2015 and adopted 20 May 2019 under the Juncker Commission (Andrus Ansip) · Part of the Digital Single Market agenda

What it does

Aims to contribute to the internal market and a high level of consumer protection by laying down common rules for contracts in which traders supply digital content or digital services to consumers, including when consumers provide personal data instead of money. It sets requirements on conformity with the contract, remedies for lack of conformity or failure to supply, and conditions for modifying content.

What we know so far

The Directive gave consumers EU-wide rights when apps, streaming, software and other digital content or services are faulty. The Commission forecast that, with the sister Sale of Goods Directive, it would add about EUR 4 billion a year to EU GDP and bring 8-13 million more people to shop online across borders. None of this has been measured: the Commission's review was due in June 2024 but has not been found, and no independent study has assessed the effects.
