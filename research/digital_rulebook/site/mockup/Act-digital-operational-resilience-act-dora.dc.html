Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA)

Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 ↗Adopted 14 Dec 2022Evidence: thin

Proposed 24 Sep 2020 and adopted 14 Dec 2022 under the first von der Leyen Commission (Margrethe Vestager, Thierry Breton) · Part of the Digital Decade agenda

What it does

Aims to achieve a high common level of digital operational resilience in the financial sector. It requires financial entities to manage ICT risk, report major ICT-related incidents, test their digital operational resilience and manage risks from ICT third-party providers, including through contract requirements. It creates an EU oversight framework for critical ICT third-party service providers and allows sharing of cyber threat information.

What we know so far

DORA aims to make banks, insurers and other financial firms withstand IT failures and cyber attacks, and to oversee their key tech suppliers. Since it applied in January 2025, regulators have mapped firms' IT supply chains and put 19 critical providers - including the big cloud companies - under direct oversight. The forecast savings and reduction in incident costs have not been measured; the only cost evidence comes from vendor surveys suggesting many firms spent over EUR 1 million on compliance.
