Medical Devices Regulation

Regulation (EU) 2017/745 ↗Adopted 5 Apr 2017Evidence: moderate

Proposed 26 Sep 2012 under the second Barroso Commission (Neelie Kroes) and adopted 5 Apr 2017 under the Juncker Commission (Andrus Ansip) · Part of the Europe 2020 agenda

What it does

Sets rules for placing medical devices (including software) on the EU market and for clinical investigations, to ensure a high level of safety and health while supporting innovation. It tightens the designation and supervision of notified bodies, requires clinical evaluation, post-market surveillance and vigilance reporting, introduces a unique device identification system and the EUDAMED database, and makes manufacturers, authorised representatives, importers and distributors responsible for compliance.

What we know so far

The Regulation has applied since May 2021, with transition periods extended several times. The Commission's 2025 targeted evaluation found safety and transparency improving but compliance costs high and often disproportionate. In surveys for the Commission's monitoring study, 48% of responding manufacturers had withdrawn some devices from the EU since 2021, most because revenue did not justify recertification, and notified bodies report that most new certificates take more than a year. The only before-and-after test found, for AI imaging devices, shows no change in the gap between EU and US approval. A targeted revision proposed in December 2025 is estimated to save about EUR 3.3 billion a year.
